Never Alone on the Way Home: Acompaña Against Street Harassment

Read the blog

By Paula Camayo & Gabriela Cocuy · Interactive Media Design

Imagine...

Imagine that you are walking home alone, and suddenly you feel someone is following you. What would you do? You can’t get your phone out of your pocket, it is difficult to ask for help without looking at your phone, and shouting could put you at greater risk.

valentina

That is exactly what happened to Valentina, a university student in Cali. She had stayed late at the library, and she was walking home when she noticed that a man had followed her for three blocks. Her phone was in her bag, and she froze.

Unfortunately, this is a situation many people face, especially women. Feeling safe walking home alone is becoming rare, and we have started to see our rights as if they were optional.

problem

The
Problem.

8 / 10women have experienced street harassment
9 / 10are afraid of suffering a sexual attack in public spaces
73%did not know where to report harassment

So, what are we really talking about? Gender-based violence is any harmful act directed at a person because of their gender, and women and girls are the most affected. One of its most common forms is street harassment: unwanted comments, gestures, touching or following by strangers in public spaces.

In our society, girls learn from a very young age a set of codes to stay safe. It is very common to see groups of friends who share their location all the time, always go to the bathroom together, or even have a secret word that means “I’m in trouble.” These strategies exist for a reason: in Bogotá, 8 out of 10 women have experienced street harassment at some point in their lives, and 9 out of 10 are afraid of suffering a sexual attack in public spaces or on public transport (Infobae, 2023). infobae

Even though this problem is widely known, it is rarely addressed, because it exposes beliefs deeply rooted in a patriarchal society. That is why victims often hear questions like: Why were you alone at that time? Why didn’t you protect yourself? Why didn’t you fight back? What did you do to provoke it?

Being on alert 24/7 is exhausting. Having to explain yourself is exhausting. That is why we created Acompaña.

acompaña

Our
Solution.

Women’s safety shouldn’t depend on which way you walk home or whether you have company. With that in mind, we came up with Acompaña, a design project that uses technology to help people, especially women, feel supported on their way home.

Acompaña works on different devices so it can be accessible for everyone: phones, smartwatches and even a special bracelet, all designed to be as fast as possible to use. From the app, you can choose your trusted contacts, share your location and find information about support centers for women.

It has two main features. The preventive alert lets you tell your trusted contacts that you are on your way and share your location during the trip. The emergency alert is activated by pressing and holding a button, and it sends a priority notification with your location to your contacts, plus quick access to Colombia’s emergency line, 123.

You can activate alerts from your phone, connect your smartwatch for a more discreet option, or use our bracelet, which you can personalize to wear every day. It is designed to be waterproof and more affordable than a smartwatch. Our goal is to make women feel accompanied before, during and after their journeys.

How We Can
Contribute.

As designers and as women, we know that technology only helps if it works in stressful moments. From Interactive Media Design, we are creating an app for different devices with a simple interface, where one gesture, like holding a button, is enough to ask for help. We also designed how the app and the bracelet work together and built a prototype to test with real users. From Communication, we wrote clear alert messages and gathered information about support centers.

Acompaña brings safety and comfort to our daily lives. This matters because, according to UN Women, fewer than 40% of women who experience violence seek help, and most turn to family and friends. Acompaña makes that first call for help faster.

phases

Feasibility
and Challenges.

Acompaña is a realistic project because it can grow in phases. First, as students, we can do the research, design the app prototype in Figma and test it with female students at Icesi, using free tools. Then, with partners such as engineering students, women’s organizations and Cali’s gender equity offices, the bracelet could be produced with technology that already exists, like GPS and Bluetooth.

Of course, there are challenges. If there is no internet, alerts can be sent by SMS. To protect privacy, the location is shared only during an active alert, following Colombia’s data protection law. And because emergency services may not respond immediately, alerts always reach trusted contacts first.

impact

Expected
Impact.

In Cali, Acompaña would help young women and students move through the city with more confidence, knowing that help is one gesture away. It also gives them clear information about where to get support, which is important because in Bogotá, 73% of women surveyed said they did not know where to report harassment. bogota

But this is not only a Colombian problem. According to UN Women, one in three women worldwide experience physical or sexual violence. Since emergency numbers and support centers can be changed in the app, Acompaña could be adapted to any country, contributing to the UN Sustainable Development Goal 5: Gender Equality. unwomen

Conclusion/ Call toAction

Gender-based violence takes away something as simple as walking home in peace. Acompaña cannot end this problem alone, but it can make sure that no woman feels alone when she needs help. We invite you to share this post, talk about this issue, and stop asking victims what they did wrong. Because walking home safely should never be a privilege.

AI-Supported
Process.

AProblem exploration with AI

During this project we mainly used ChatGPT as a support tool, not as a writer. First, we asked it to explain step by step how a blog like this should be built, and we followed that structure to write our own text. We also used it to find sources and statistics about street harassment and gender-based violence, and to look for similar projects. Later, we asked it for ideas on how to approach our solution and for possible names, and near the end we asked it to review our project. AI never wrote our blog, and it didn’t tell us anything about our project idea. Finally, we used AI (Claude) to help us build the HTML page where the blog is published, not to write its content.

BPrompting process
Prompt 1 · Blog structure“We have to write a blog for our English class that proposes a solution to a social problem. Can you explain step by step how we should build it, and what is expected in each part?”
Prompt 2 · Sources“We’re working on a university project about street harassment and women’s safety in Cali, Colombia. Can you help us find reliable sources with statistics about this? We’d also like to know if there are similar projects in other countries. Please include the links so we can check them.”
Prompt 3 · Ideas & name“We want to design an app and a bracelet that help women feel safe when they walk home alone. What are some ways we could approach this idea? Could you also suggest a few names for the project?”
Prompt 4 · Review“This is our project: Acompaña, an app and a bracelet that let women send preventive and emergency alerts to their trusted contacts. Can you review it and tell us what’s missing, what isn’t clear, or what problems we could have if we tried to do it in real life?”

At first, we only asked general questions about how to build the blog. The AI explained each part, for example, that the introduction needs a hook and has to present the problem, and we wrote each part ourselves following that guide. Later, our prompts became more specific: we gave more context (the city, the problem, what we study) and asked directly for links, so we could check the answers.

CCritical evaluation

The most useful parts were the step-by-step structure, the sources, and the review of our project. Not everything was useful: the AI gave us many name ideas, but most of them were too long or complicated, so we kept the first name that came up, Acompaña, because it was simple and clear. Every time the AI gave us a source, we opened the link and checked that the information really came from that website and that the numbers matched. If we couldn’t find it in the original source, we didn’t use it.

DHuman contribution

We wrote the whole blog ourselves. AI only helped us with the structure, with finding sources, and with building the web page. We made all the important decisions together: the problem, how Acompaña works, its name, and which sources to trust. Our discipline, Interactive Media Design, is the base of the proposal: it taught us to design for the user, to create simple interfaces, and to prototype and test with real people. We also know our local context, so we adapted the general ideas to the reality of women in Cali.

EEthical reflection

AI can be very helpful, but it can also give false information or invent sources, so it must be checked. It can also reflect bias, because it gives general answers that don’t always fit a specific community. For us, using AI responsibly means using it as a guide and never letting it replace our own thinking or our own voice.
